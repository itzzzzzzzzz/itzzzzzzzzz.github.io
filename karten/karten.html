1

Hello everyone. My presentation is about Wyoming, the 44th state of the USA. Wyoming covers 97,813 square miles, which is about 253,000 square kilometres. That is about 70 percent of the size of Germany. ⁠

2

Wyoming lies in the west of the USA, in the Rocky Mountains and the High Plains. It is the 10th largest state, but it has the fewest people of all states: about 587,000 in 2024. The capital and largest city is Cheyenne. I will talk about Wyoming's history, why it is called the Equality State, its nature, and why it is called the Cowboy State. ⁠

3

First, the history of Wyoming, in three steps. ⁠

4

Long before the first settlers came, the Shoshone, Arapaho, Crow, Cheyenne and Lakota lived on this land. From the 1840s to 1869, about 400,000 people travelled west through Wyoming on the Oregon, California and Mormon trails. Most of them crossed the Rocky Mountains at South Pass, the easiest way over the mountains. ⁠

5

In 1867 the Union Pacific Railroad reached Cheyenne. Along the railroad, new towns such as Laramie and Rock Springs grew up. On July 25, 1868, Congress created the Wyoming Territory from parts of the Dakota, Utah and Idaho territories. ⁠

6

On July 10, 1890, Wyoming became the 44th state of the USA. It was also the first state in which women were allowed to vote. ⁠

7

Wyoming's nickname is the Equality State. The name comes from its history: in 1869, Wyoming was one of the first places in the world where women could vote. In the whole USA, women could only vote from 1920, 51 years later. The state motto is "Equal Rights". ⁠

This picture shows the Teton Range and the Snake River in Grand Teton National Park. The highest peak is the Grand Teton, 4,199 metres high. It is the second highest mountain in Wyoming; only Gannett Peak in the Wind River Range is a few metres higher. The mountains rise more than 2,000 metres above the valley, which is called Jackson Hole. ⁠

9

This is Old Faithful, a geyser in Yellowstone National Park. The people at the bottom show how high the water shoots up: about every 60 to 110 minutes, up to 56 metres. Yellowstone became the first national park in the world in 1872, and about 96 percent of it lies in Wyoming. The park has about half of all geysers on Earth. Their water is heated by a large volcano under the park. ⁠

10

This picture shows an American bison next to the frozen Yellowstone Lake. Bison are the largest land animals in North America; a male can weigh up to 900 kilograms. In the 1800s they were hunted until only about 1,000 were left. Today about 5,000 bison live in Yellowstone. The bison is Wyoming's state mammal and is on the state flag. ⁠

11

This is Devils Tower in the northeast of Wyoming. It is a tower of volcanic rock, 264 metres high from its base. The long columns on its sides formed when hot magma cooled down. In 1906 it became the first national monument of the USA. ⁠

12

This picture shows bronc riding at Cheyenne Frontier Days: a cowboy tries to stay on a bucking horse. Frontier Days has been held every July since 1897 and is one of the largest rodeos in the world. Wyoming is called the Cowboy State because ranching and rodeo are a big part of its history. There are about twice as many cattle as people in Wyoming. ⁠
