Hello everyone. This presentation is about Wyoming, the 44th state of the USA. Wyoming covers 97,813 square miles, which is about 253,000 square kilometres, or roughly 70 percent of the area of Germany.

Wyoming lies in the west of the USA, in the Rocky Mountains and the High Plains. It is the 10th largest state, but it has the fewest people of all states: about 587,000 in 2024. The capital and largest city is Cheyenne. The presentation has four parts: the history of Wyoming, the vote for women, its nature, and the Cowboy State.

First, the history of Wyoming, in three steps.

Long before the first settlers came, the Shoshone, Arapaho, Crow, Cheyenne and Lakota lived on this land. From the 1840s to 1869, about 400,000 people crossed Wyoming on the Oregon, California and Mormon trails. The easiest way over the Rocky Mountains was South Pass. Travelers tried to reach Independence Rock by July 4, so that they could cross the mountains before winter. Thousands of them carved their names into the rock.

In November 1867 the Union Pacific Railroad reached Cheyenne, a town that had been founded only that July. Along the railroad, towns such as Laramie and Rock Springs grew up. On July 25, 1868, Congress created the Wyoming Territory from parts of the Dakota, Utah and Idaho territories.

On July 10, 1890, Wyoming became the 44th state of the USA, and the first state in which women could vote. Some members of Congress wanted Wyoming to give up the women's vote before it joined. Wyoming's lawmakers are reported to have answered that they would rather stay out of the Union for a hundred years than come in without the women.

This brings us to the vote for women. On December 10, 1869, Governor John A. Campbell signed a law of the territory's first legislature: women in Wyoming could vote and hold office. It was the first such law in the USA. In 1870 Esther Hobart Morris became the first woman justice of the peace in the USA, and in Laramie women served on a jury. On September 6, 1870, Louisa Swain became the first woman in the USA to vote in a general election.

Each step of the staircase shows when women won the vote in another country. New Zealand was the first country, in 1893. Australia followed in 1902, Finland in 1906 and Germany in 1918. In the whole USA, women could only vote from 1920, after the 19th Amendment: 51 years after Wyoming. France followed in 1944, Switzerland in 1971 and Saudi Arabia in 2015. This is why Wyoming is called the Equality State. Its motto is "Equal Rights".

Now five pictures. The first was taken by Ansel Adams in 1942: the Tetons and the Snake River in Grand Teton National Park. The highest peak, Grand Teton, is 13,775 feet high, about 4,200 metres. In 1977 this photograph was put on the Golden Record of the two Voyager space probes, as one of 115 pictures of life on Earth. Voyager 1 left the solar system in 2012 and is the most distant object ever made by people.

The second picture shows Old Faithful in Yellowstone. William Henry Jackson took it in 1872 for the government's Hayden survey. His photographs from the year before had helped convince Congress, and on March 1, 1872, Yellowstone became the first national park in the world. 96 percent of the park lies in Wyoming. Old Faithful erupts about every 60 to 110 minutes, up to 56 metres high. Yellowstone lies on a supervolcano. Its last large eruption was about 631,000 years ago.

The third picture shows a bison by the frozen Yellowstone Lake in 1940. There were once tens of millions of bison in North America. They were hunted until only about a thousand were left at the end of the 1880s. In 1902 fewer than two dozen wild bison lived in Yellowstone. Today about 5,000 live there. The bison is Wyoming's state mammal, it is on the state flag, and since 2016 it has been the national mammal of the USA.

The fourth picture is Devils Tower, photographed by N. H. Darton in 1900. It is a tower of volcanic rock with columns on its sides, 867 feet high, about 264 metres. In 1906 President Theodore Roosevelt made it the first national monument of the USA. Many Plains nations call it Bear Lodge. In a Kiowa story, a giant bear left the lines on its sides with its claws. In 1977 it was a filming location of Steven Spielberg's film "Close Encounters of the Third Kind".

The last picture shows bronc riding at Cheyenne Frontier Days, around 1910. Frontier Days has been held every July since 1897 and is the largest outdoor rodeo in the world. A bucking horse and rider has been on Wyoming's license plates since 1936 and is a registered trademark of the state. Wyoming is called the Cowboy State.
