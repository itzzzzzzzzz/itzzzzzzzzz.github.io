# Wyoming: speaker notes

## 1. Opening: WYOMING, 97,813 sq mi
Wyoming is the 44th state of the USA. It covers 97,813 square miles (253,335 km²).

## 2. Introduction
Wyoming lies in the west of the USA, in the Rocky Mountains and the High Plains. It is the 10th largest state but has the fewest people: about 587,000 (2024). The capital and largest city is Cheyenne.
Four parts: its history, the vote for women, its nature, and the Cowboy State.

## History

### 3. The history of Wyoming, in three steps

### 4. 1840s: the trails west
- Long before settlers came, Shoshone, Arapaho, Crow, Cheyenne and Lakota lived here.
- From the 1840s to 1869, about 400,000 people crossed Wyoming on the Oregon, California and Mormon trails. South Pass was the easiest way over the Rocky Mountains.
- Travelers tried to reach Independence Rock by July 4, to cross the mountains before winter. Thousands of names carved into it can still be read.

### 5. 1868: Wyoming becomes a territory
- The Union Pacific Railroad reached Cheyenne in November 1867. The town had been founded only that July.
- On July 25, 1868, Congress created the Wyoming Territory from parts of the Dakota, Utah and Idaho territories.

### 6. 1890: Wyoming becomes a state
- On July 10, 1890, Wyoming joined the Union as the 44th state, and the first state where women could vote.
- Some members of Congress wanted Wyoming to drop the women's vote first. Wyoming's lawmakers are reported to have answered that they would rather stay out of the Union for a hundred years than come in without the women.

## The vote for women

### 7. The staircase: Wyoming took the first step
- On December 10, 1869, Governor John A. Campbell signed the law of the territory's first legislature: women could vote and hold office. It was the first such law in the USA.
- 1870: Esther Hobart Morris of South Pass City became the first woman justice of the peace in the USA. In Laramie, women served on a jury, among the first anywhere.
- September 6, 1870: Louisa Swain of Laramie was the first woman in the USA to vote in a general election.
- 1925: Nellie Tayloe Ross became governor of Wyoming, the first woman governor of a US state.
- The steps above Wyoming show when women won the vote elsewhere:
  - New Zealand 1893: the first country.
  - Australia 1902.
  - Finland 1906: the first in Europe, and women could also be elected.
  - Germany 1918.
  - The whole USA in 1920 (19th Amendment), 51 years after Wyoming.
  - France 1944.
  - Switzerland 1971.
  - Saudi Arabia 2015.
  How many steps show depends on the size of the screen. The main ones always show.
- That is why Wyoming is called the Equality State. Its motto is "Equal Rights".

## Five pictures

### 8. The Tetons and the Snake River (Ansel Adams, 1942)
- Ansel Adams took this photograph for the National Park Service. It shows Grand Teton National Park (since 1929). The highest peak, Grand Teton, is 13,775 ft (4,199 m) high.
- In 1977 this photograph was put on the Golden Record of the two Voyager space probes, as one of 115 pictures of life on Earth for anyone who might find them.
- Voyager 1 left the solar system in 2012 and Voyager 2 in 2018. Voyager 1 is the most distant object ever made by people, and it carries this picture of Wyoming.

### 9. Old Faithful (William Henry Jackson, 1872)
- William Henry Jackson photographed Yellowstone for the government's Hayden survey. His pictures helped convince Congress, and on March 1, 1872, Yellowstone became the first national park in the world. 96 percent of it lies in Wyoming.
- Old Faithful erupts about every 60 to 110 minutes, up to 185 ft (56 m) high. Note the visitors at its foot.
- Yellowstone has about half of all the geysers on Earth. Under it lies a supervolcano. Its last great eruption was about 631,000 years ago.

### 10. A bison by the frozen Yellowstone Lake (1940)
- There were once tens of millions of bison in North America. By the end of the 1880s only about a thousand were left. In 1902 fewer than two dozen wild bison lived in Yellowstone.
- Today about 5,000 live there. Yellowstone is the only place in the USA where bison have lived without a break since prehistoric times.
- The bison is Wyoming's state mammal and is on its flag. Since 2016 it has been the national mammal of the USA.
- The lake in the background is Yellowstone Lake, frozen from December to about June.

### 11. Devils Tower (N. H. Darton, 1900)
- A tower of volcanic rock with columns on its sides, 867 ft (264 m) high from its base.
- In 1906 President Theodore Roosevelt made it the first national monument of the USA.
- Many Plains nations call it Bear Lodge. In a Kiowa story, seven girls fled from a giant bear onto a rock that grew into the sky. The bear's claws left the lines on its sides, and the girls became the stars of the Pleiades.
- In 1977 it was the landing place of the aliens in Steven Spielberg's film "Close Encounters of the Third Kind".

### 12. Bronc riding at Cheyenne Frontier Days (around 1910)
- Cheyenne Frontier Days has been held every July since 1897. It is called "the Daddy of 'em All", the largest outdoor rodeo in the world.
- A bucking horse and rider has been on Wyoming's license plates since 1936, and it is the state's trademark. Wyoming is called the Cowboy State.

### 13. Last card: the bison with the state seal
- The state seal carries the words "Equal Rights".
